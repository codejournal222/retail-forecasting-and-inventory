Four non-overlapping validation horizons choose parameters by MAE; model selection also uses validation only. The 90th percentile of signed cumulative validation errors (actual minus predicted), floored at zero, provides a safety buffer. With only four calibration origins this is an illustrative buffer, not a demonstrated 90% service guarantee. The same validation sample chooses parameters and estimates the buffer, so calibration is exploratory rather than independent.

Test windows are separate from validation. Forecasts are generated at each review using only prior mature history, then recursively predict multiple days without access to intervening actuals. A 1-day reporting delay allows cancellation labels to mature. Earlier test observations may enter later training only once available. Product models use calendar lags 1, 7 and 28 and shifted rolling features; Random Forest month/weekday variables are known at forecast time.

5. Inventory experiment

At the start of each day, receive scheduled stock, review inventory when due, place an order, then fulfil demand. An order arrives after 7 calendar days. The order-up-to target covers lead time plus review interval, with a model-specific validation buffer. Inventory position includes on-order stock. All models start each test window with the same stock based on prior sales; there is no initial pipeline. Unfulfilled demand is lost, not backordered. Historical demand is assumed observable after each day, even when the simulated policy loses sales; real lost demand may be unobserved.

Illustrative annual holding rate 20%; shortage penalty 50% of a training-only selling-price proxy per lost unit; fixed order cost £5.00. Purchase cost, revenue/profit, terminal liquidation and actual supplier constraints are excluded. Final stock and outstanding orders are reported so the finite-window boundary remains visible. The experiment is a policy comparison, not a complete profit calculation.

7. What can and cannot be concluded

The findings are based on a limited sample of products and approximately one year of data. Recorded sales may understate demand if products were out of stock, but historical stock availability is unknown. Cancellation matching relies on assumptions about related transactions, and safety-stock estimates use only a few validation periods. Simpler forecasting methods performed well in this comparison, highlighting the importance of testing machine-learning models against basic alternatives. Assessing whether these findings apply more broadly would require additional products, several years of data, costs that reflect actual business operations, and a verified trading calendar.
